> "The benefit is that small teams gain independence of action, enabling them to work and experiment on more manageable parts of the problem in parallel and more quickly and safely, with lower costs of coordination."
>
> Gene Kim and Steven J. Spear, *Wiring the Winning Organization*

## What it means

Independence of action means people can solve the problems in front of them, and change how they work, without asking permission or coordinating with the rest of the org. Kim and Spear treat it as a structural property, not a cultural one. You don't get it by declaring people empowered. You get it by wiring the work so independence is possible.

It is the payoff of simplification, one of the book's three mechanisms (slowification, simplification, amplification). Two of its techniques create it directly:

- **Modularization** splits a system into coherent pieces joined by a few stable, well-defined interfaces. A team can change what happens inside its module without negotiating across the boundary. Independence for work done in parallel.
- **Linearization** gives each workflow dedicated resources and defined handoffs, so sequential work moves without re-negotiating every step. Independence for work done in series. In the authors' words, linearization does for sequential processes what modularization does for parallel ones.

Two conditions keep it working:

- Problems stay contained inside the module. In the book's hotel vignette, managers kept pulling painters from other rooms whenever one room fell behind, which re-coupled the teams and let local problems spread. The fix was a reserve team, so trouble got absorbed without breaking everyone else's independence.
- Someone minds the whole. Leaders own the balance between independence of action and enough interface compatibility that the parts still add up to one coherent system.

## Why it matters

Coordination is the tax that stalls large organizations. When every problem needs a meeting, ingenuity sits idle in queues. Wire for independence instead, and many problems get solved at once, by the people closest to them, with fast feedback. That is the "liberating our collective greatness" from the book's subtitle.
